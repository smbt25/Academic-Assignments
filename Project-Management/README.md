# Biodegradable Packaging for a Retail Supermarket  
### A Sustainability Implementation Project for Tesco Corstorphine Extra

This is the group project of **Group 21** for the module **C11PA Project Management**, completed as part of the **MSc in Finance and Management** at **Edinburgh Business School, Heriot-Watt University**.

The project proposes an eight-month pilot to introduce **certified seaweed-based compostable packaging** for 20 high-rotation ready-to-eat own-brand products at **Tesco Corstorphine Extra, Edinburgh**. It aims to reduce single-use plastic packaging while maintaining product availability, food safety, shelf-life performance, and customer satisfaction.

The proposal is designed as an operational implementation project rather than a material-development project. It covers supplier selection, packaging design and approval, EN 13432 certification and regulatory compliance, store rollout, staff training, and post-implementation monitoring.

**Microsoft Project** was used to develop the project schedule, work breakdown structure, resource assignments, critical path, and Gantt chart. These planning tools supported milestone tracking, dependency management, and schedule control across the project lifecycle.

## Project Objectives

- Implement compostable packaging across 20 selected ready-to-eat SKUs within eight months.
- Reduce conventional single-use plastic packaging across the selected products by at least 60% by weight.
- Maintain product availability above 90% and customer satisfaction above 90% during the pilot.
- Achieve at least a 95% packaging-performance success rate during the first three months of rollout.
- Keep incremental packaging costs within a 10% variance of current packaging costs.
- Ensure compliance with EN 13432 industrial compostability standards and UK food-contact regulations.


## Project Management Approach

The project applies a structured project-management framework including:

- Project scope definition and boundary control  
- Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)  
- Cost Breakdown Structure (CBS)  
- Gantt chart, milestones, and critical-path analysis  
- Stakeholder power–interest analysis  
- RACI responsibility matrix  
- Risk register, risk scoring, and monitoring framework  
- Communication plan and governance structure  
- Project monitoring dashboard and closure report  

The project has a total budget of **£1.5 million**, including a **10% contingency reserve** of £150,000. Five formal milestone gates are built into the schedule to ensure that each phase is reviewed and approved before the next phase begins.

## Conclusions

This project proposes a practical and controlled approach to reducing single-use plastic packaging in a high-volume retail category. By piloting certified seaweed-based compostable packaging across 20 ready-to-eat SKUs at Tesco Corstorphine Extra, the project tests whether a sustainable packaging alternative can be introduced without compromising product availability, food safety, shelf life, customer satisfaction, or operational efficiency.

The project is designed to deliver more than a packaging change. It creates a structured implementation plan, a measurable monitoring framework, and evidence that can inform a wider rollout decision. Success is assessed across environmental, technical, financial, operational, and customer-related criteria.

The key expected conclusions are:

- Certified compostable packaging can be deployed in a live retail environment within an eight-month project lifecycle.
- The pilot can reduce conventional single-use plastic packaging across selected SKUs by at least 60% by weight.
- Packaging performance, including durability, moisture resistance, and shelf-life preservation, can meet the required quality threshold.
- The project can remain within its £1.5 million budget, supported by a 10% contingency reserve.
- Customer satisfaction and product availability can be maintained above 90% during the pilot.
- The results will provide evidence on whether the approach is suitable for scaling to other Tesco stores and product categories.

## Practical Use

The project has practical value for Tesco, suppliers, store teams, customers, and waste-management partners.

For **Tesco**, it provides a controlled way to test compostable packaging before committing to a wider rollout. The pilot generates evidence on cost, supplier reliability, packaging performance, customer acceptance, and operational feasibility. This supports evidence-based decisions on whether to expand the initiative across more stores or product categories.

For **store operations**, the project includes staff training, supply-chain integration, and clear implementation procedures. This helps reduce disruption during the transition and ensures that staff understand how to handle, store, and replenish products using the new packaging.

For **procurement and sustainability teams**, the project provides a structured supplier-selection and compliance process. It also creates measurable KPIs on plastic reduction, certification, packaging defects, cost variance, and customer feedback.

For **customers**, the project offers a visible sustainability improvement while maintaining the quality and availability of ready-to-eat products. Clear sustainability labelling can also improve transparency around the environmental impact of packaging.

For **waste-management partners**, the project highlights the importance of ensuring that compostable packaging can be processed effectively at end of life. The environmental benefit depends not only on replacing plastic but also on the availability of suitable composting infrastructure.

## Limitations

The project has several limitations that should be considered when interpreting its results.

- **Pilot-scale evidence:** The project covers only 20 ready-to-eat SKUs in one Tesco store. Results may not automatically apply to all product categories, store formats, or locations.
- **Supplier dependency:** The project assumes that a supplier can deliver certified seaweed-based packaging at the required scale, quality, and price. Supplier delays or material variability could affect the schedule and budget.
- **Technical performance risk:** Seaweed-based packaging may be sensitive to moisture and humidity. If it does not perform as well as conventional plastic packaging, this could affect product protection, shelf life, and customer acceptance.
- **Certification and regulation:** EN 13432 certification and UK food-contact compliance are essential before rollout. Because these processes are externally controlled, delays could affect the overall project timeline.
- **Waste-infrastructure dependency:** The environmental benefit of compostable packaging depends on customers disposing of it correctly and on the availability of appropriate industrial composting facilities.
- **Cost constraints:** The £1.5 million budget, including a £150,000 contingency reserve, limits flexibility. Higher material costs, re-testing, or supplier changes could create financial pressure.
- **Short monitoring period:** The pilot runs for eight months. Longer-term effects, such as durability over extended storage periods, customer behaviour changes, and full-scale cost efficiency, would require further evaluation.
- **Assumption of stable conditions:** The plan assumes stable regulations, supplier performance, customer acceptance, and internal cooperation from Tesco teams. Significant changes in any of these areas could affect delivery.

## Future Recommendations

If the pilot meets its environmental, operational, and financial targets, the next step would be to evaluate a phased rollout across additional Tesco stores and ready-to-eat product categories. Future work should also assess long-term packaging durability, supplier scalability, customer disposal behaviour, and the availability of composting infrastructure.

Further analysis should compare the total cost of compostable packaging with conventional plastic packaging at larger volumes, as economies of scale may improve cost competitiveness. Finally, customer feedback and sales data should be monitored beyond the pilot period to understand whether the packaging change has any lasting effect on purchasing behaviour.
