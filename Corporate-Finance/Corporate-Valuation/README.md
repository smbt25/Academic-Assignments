# Pershing Square Holdings (PSH.L): Valuation Report & Analysis

## Executive Summary
This report undertakes a comprehensive valuation of **Pershing Square Holdings, Ltd. (PSH.L)**, a Guernsey-registered closed-ended investment trust managed by Pershing Square Capital Management. PSH executes an activist investment strategy focused on a concentrated portfolio of large-capitalization North American equities. Because traditional cash flow and dividend models are heavily distorted by accounting volatility and low payout ratios, **Net Asset Value (NAV)** serves as the primary and most reliable valuation framework for assessing the fund's intrinsic worth.

---

## Business Profile & Strategy
* **Corporate Structure:** Guernsey-incorporated closed-ended investment company listed on the London Stock Exchange (LSE) and FTSE 100.
* **Investment Strategy:** Concentrated portfolio of roughly 15 core North American large-cap holdings (e.g., Alphabet, Amazon, Chipotle, Hilton, Uber) combined with an activist approach and hedging strategies.
* **Tax Status:** Tax-exempt at the corporate level in Guernsey; subject only to US withholding taxes.
* **Payout & Retention:** Maintains a low dividend payout ratio (~4%), retaining approximately 96% of earnings for reinvestment into market opportunities.

---

## Methodology & Cost of Capital
* **Risk-Free Rate:** Tracked using a 10-year monthly Gilt Rate series.
* **Cost of Equity ($K_e$):** 
  * **CAPM:** Calculated at **8.57%** (using median data to mitigate pandemic distortions).
  * **Fama-French 3-Factor (FF3):** Calculated at **11.5%** (reflecting a growth orientation and negative SMB/HML betas consistent with large-cap growth holdings).
* **Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC):** Estimated at **9.97%** using the FF3 model framework.
* **Growth Rates:** Short-term growth estimated at 6.49% based on ROE (6.77%) and retention ratio (96%); long-term nominal growth benchmarks set at 4.00% (US) and 3.6% (UK).

---

## Valuation Model Comparison
1. **Dividend Discount Model (DDM):** Yields intrinsic values of $15.57 (CAPM) and $9.74 (FF3), significantly below the market price ($48.67). *Conclusion:* Unsuitable for PSH due to the low dividend payout ratio and high reinvestment rate.
2. **FCFF & FCFE Models:** Produces unrealistically large enterprise and equity valuations due to perpetuity formula sensitivity. *Conclusion:* Inappropriate because investment income and realized/unrealized gains are irregular and fail to capture NAV appreciation.
3. **Market-Based Valuation:** Compares PSH against peers (e.g., Scottish Mortgage, Intermediate Capital Group) across multiples. *Conclusion:* Unreliable due to distortions from unrealized gains/losses in accounting standards, though PSH appears undervalued across certain book value metrics.
4. **Residual Income Valuation (RIV):** Single-stage RIV gives $45.37 (CAPM) and $28.56 (FF3); two-stage RIV suggests $71.06. *Conclusion:* Unreliable due to volatility in reported earnings and ROE driven by portfolio market fluctuations.
5. **Net Asset Value (NAV) Valuation:** The definitive model for PSH. NAV-based forecasting yields implied prices close to current market levels ($48.67), indicating that PSH trades at a modest premium/fair value relative to its historical discount trends.

---

## Conclusions
* **NAV is the Definitive Metric:** For closed-ended investment funds with activist strategies, cash flow and dividend-based models fail. Intrinsic value is fundamentally tied to the fair market value of the underlying portfolio assets.
* **Valuation Status:** PSH is currently evaluated as fairly valued to modestly overvalued relative to its NAV dynamics, trading at an observed discount of approximately 27% to its net asset value.
* **Growth Over Yield:** The company’s strategy relies heavily on capital appreciation and reinvestment rather than dividend distributions, making earnings retention a key driver of long-term value creation.

---

## Practical Implications
1. **Appropriate Valuation Frameworks:** Financial analysts and investors must avoid applying standard corporate DDM or DCF models to investment trusts with low dividend payout ratios and volatile investment portfolios, relying instead on asset-based NAV analysis.
2. **Navigating Closed-Ended Discounts:** Investors must account for structural discounts to NAV, which are driven by market sentiment, liquidity constraints, and fee structures rather than underlying asset performance alone.
3. **Managing Accounting Volatility:** Because investment trust earnings are heavily influenced by unrealized gains and losses, risk management and performance evaluation should focus on multi-period NAV trends rather than single-year accounting net income.
